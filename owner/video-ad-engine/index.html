Core purpose: attention → curiosity → book recognition → sample/purchase intent → series return. Current explicit instruction and locked canon always outrank the engine.

Operating rule

Canon beats spectacle.

Before an ad is written, check the current manuscript or Book Master Record, current cover, illustrations, character portraits, objects, maps, and series positioning. Never replace a locked face, weapon, symbol, supernatural light, or book-specific object with generic fantasy imagery merely because it looks dramatic.

Default 15-second architecture

Hook. Prove. Escalate. Promise. Close.

0.0–1.5 · STOP

Immediate hook plus a fast title/book identity flash. The first frame must work muted.

1.5–5.0 · PROVE THE WORLD

Two or three concrete story details. Objects, rules, danger, place—not generic genre claims.

5.0–9.5 · ESCALATE

Reveal that the threat is larger, smarter, stranger, or more organized than it first appeared.

9.5–12.5 · PROMISE

State the distinctive book proposition or standalone/series-gateway promise.

12.5–15.0 · CLOSE

Full current cover, author identity, and one primary call to action.

Early identity / delayed full reveal

Use a quick book/title identifier in the first 1–2 seconds, then return to story. Save the full selling reveal for the close.

Parker hook law

If it could sell 500 unrelated fantasy books, rewrite it.

Preferred hooks are concrete danger, a strange rule, a physical object carrying consequence, a contradiction, a place behaving wrongly, or a short question with a cost. Avoid slow logos, explanatory lore, and series numbers before the premise.

The Moon That Hunts · locked learning case

The horror gateway into High Pass.

Dogs are killed first.
Bells carry lies.
Names spoken after dark become bait.

Escalation: It is testing. It is counting. It is teaching.

Gateway promise: A complete standalone story. No prior reading required.

Lesson: Do not advertise “Book Five” first. Advertise the danger, identify the book early, then tell the reader it stands alone.

Visual + sound laws

Specific beats generic.

Motion

Restrained push-ins, slow pans, clean parallax, environmental motion, purposeful cuts. Avoid long dead holds and frantic random transitions. A 15-second acquisition beat should rarely remain completely unchanged for more than about 1.5–2 seconds unless stillness is intentional.

Sound

Low strings, restrained drones, meaningful object sounds, sparse percussion, clean final impact. For Moon: cold low pulse → restrained bell → rising pressure → cover impact. Every ad must still work muted.

Text

One idea per card. Keep critical copy clear of lower/right app UI. Never shrink text just to fit more words. Premise before series metadata.

CTA

One ad = one primary action: Start Here, Read Chapter One, Buy the Paperback, Enter The Lantern Road, Watch the Full Film, or Continue the Cycle.

Testing + measurement

One test should answer one question.

For A/B tests, keep the body and close constant and change one meaningful variable—usually the first 1–2 seconds. Track first 1–3 second hold, average watch time, completion, rewatches, saves, shares, story-interest comments, profile visits, site clicks, retailer clicks, purchases when available, and cost per qualified reader action. Do not optimize for impressions alone.

Pre-publish test

Ten questions.

  1. Does the first second earn the second?
  2. Does the ad clearly belong to this exact book?
  3. Is there a memorable physical object, place, law, or consequence?
  4. Are character and object details canon-correct?
  5. Can the ad be understood muted?
  6. Is book identity visible early enough to be remembered?
  7. Does the middle escalate rather than repeat?
  8. Is the full cover readable at the end?
  9. Is there only one primary CTA?
  10. Does the ad create a reader path rather than noise?

Change control

Learn without drifting.

Future performance learning should record the platform/date, creative variant, tested variable, metric change, decision, and whether the observation becomes permanent. One successful post does not automatically become a universal rule.
