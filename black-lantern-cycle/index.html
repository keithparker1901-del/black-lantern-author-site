The series promise

The Black Lantern reveals agreements no one remembers making.

It does not solve darkness with brighter magic. It exposes who wrote the rule, who benefited from it, and who was forced to pay.

Object first. Human cost next.

Each haunting is built around physical proof: a guest book, a warm silver spoon, a blackened applewood cup, a name-board, a household register, a fascicle that revises itself. The object reveals the law. The law reveals the person it has been consuming.

Witness rather than ownership.

Cael’s work is not to rescue strangers into gratitude. His burden is to keep the wrong visible long enough for the harmed person to choose what happens next.

Beauty must answer for its cost.

The cycle’s great danger is not ugliness. It is beautiful cruelty: hospitality that keeps the guest, mercy that steals grief, and elegant writing that replaces a life.
